The Problem We Were Called to Solve

Mountain Terrain. Five-Star Expectations.

Bejaan is not a city-centre property with a nearby exchange and clean horizontal cable runs. It is a multi-building luxury resort set into mountain terrain at altitude,where conventional copper degrades, wireless reflects off rock faces, and maintaining active electronics in remote risers is simply not viable.

01 Mountain Terrain Defeating Conventional Cabling

The resort's topography, split across multiple levels with long cable runs through rock, elevation changes, and two rivers adjacent to the site,made traditional Cat copper distribution unworkable for the entire property. Signal attenuation, moisture ingress, and the physical challenge of running copper through mountain-cut pathways demanded a fundamentally different distribution medium.

02 Maintenance Inaccessibility in Remote Risers

Legacy hospitality networks rely on active electronics, switches, amplifiers, media converters,distributed throughout building risers. In a remote mountain resort with limited on-site technical staff, a failing active component in a riser at 2am during peak occupancy is a material guest experience crisis. The infrastructure needed to be fully passive between the head-end and every room.

03 Guest Services Fragmented Across Five Contact Points

Luxury guests demand one elegant interface for all services, dining orders, spa bookings, room service, transport, and do-not-disturb. Operating these through disconnected phone extensions, paper menus, and separate concierge calls created service delays and an experience inconsistent with the Bejaan brand's five-star positioning in Pakistan's premium travel market.

04 VoIP Clarity Degraded by Cable Length and Altitude

IP telephony requires low-latency, jitter-free connectivity for professional voice quality. The terrain-driven cable lengths and altitude-related temperature extremes on copper produced unacceptable voice quality across guest rooms and back-of-house extensions. A new transport medium carrying converged data, voice, and IPTV over a single strand was the only viable solution.

The IES Solution Architecture

IES Engineering designed and deployed a complete GPON backbone across the entire Bejaan resort complex, a single-mode optical fibre infrastructure carrying converged data, VoIP, and IPTV to all 110 rooms over a completely passive distribution plant. One strand of glass replaces kilometres of copper, with zero active electronics between the server room and the guest room.

Single-Mode Fibre, Full Resort

OS2 fibre from OLT to every room with passive splitters, zero active electronics, immune to EMI and temperature.

  • Zero attenuation, glass carries signal 20km
  • Zero active components in risers, nothing to fail
  • Immune to EMI, glass has no electrical signal

Why this matters: A single strand of OS2 glass fibre replaces three separate copper systems (data, voice, television) with one converged passive network. The entire distribution plant from OLT to ONU contains zero active electronics — no switches, no amplifiers, nothing that consumes power or can fail in remote mountain risers.

Technology Stack

Three Platforms. One Seamless Experience.

Each technology was chosen to solve a specific constraint of the Chitral mountain environment — passive optical for terrain resilience, enterprise VoIP for clarity at elevation, and Nevron IPTV for the converged guest service layer.

Optical Distribution Layer

GPON Passive Optical Network

GPON uses single-mode glass fibre to carry converged services — data, voice, and IPTV — over one strand with no active electronics in the distribution path. For Bejaan's mountain terrain this means cable runs of hundreds of metres with zero signal degradation, zero maintenance points in risers, and a network inherently immune to the electromagnetic interference that degrades copper at altitude.

2.5 Gbps downstream / 1.25 Gbps upstream per OLT port
Max 1:128 split ratio — 20km OLT to ONU reach
Zero active components in entire distribution plant
Voice Communications Layer

Enterprise VoIP & IP Telephony

IP telephony across the GPON backbone delivers crystal-clear voice to every room and back-of-house extension. SIP trunking over the optical network achieves the jitter and packet-loss thresholds required for professional voice quality — regardless of the cable length or mountain terrain between handset and server room. MOS scores consistently exceed 4.4 (toll quality).

SIP over GPON — G.711 / G.729 codec support
<10ms jitter end-to-end — MOS 4.4+ toll quality
110 rooms + all back-of-house extensions
