The Problem

When CPU Becomes the Bottleneck at Scale

Modern banking AI doesn't fail on model accuracy — it fails on latency. A world-class fraud detection model is worthless if it returns results four minutes after a transaction clears. This was the operational reality facing one of Pakistan's most systemically important banks.

01CPU-Saturated Query Engine

The bank's analytical workloads — credit scoring, anti-money laundering scans, and predictive risk models — ran on a multi-threaded CPU cluster. At peak hours with 800 million transaction records in scope, even parallelised CPU threads reached saturation, pushing complex join and aggregation queries well beyond four minutes per execution.

02Batch Processing Masking Real-Time Risk

Because real-time AI inference was computationally prohibitive on CPU infrastructure, the bank processed fraud signals in nightly batch cycles. This 12-to-18 hour lag meant fraudulent activity was identified retroactively — after funds had already moved — rather than being intercepted at the moment of transaction.

03Model Inference Throughput Wall

The bank's data science team had built sophisticated gradient-boosted and deep-learning models achieving over 96% fraud-detection accuracy in testing. Yet deploying these models in production against the live transaction stream proved impossible — the CPU inference pipeline could not sustain the throughput required without impacting core banking performance.

04Regulatory Pressure for Real-Time Compliance

Evolving SBP (State Bank of Pakistan) directives and FATF compliance obligations began demanding near-real-time transaction monitoring, AML screening, and suspicious activity reporting with tightening SLA windows. The bank's existing architecture was architecturally incapable of meeting these mandates without a fundamental infrastructure overhaul.
