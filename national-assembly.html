The Problem We Were Called to Solve

Irreplaceable Records. Unacceptable Risk.

Pakistan's legislative institutions are custodians of the nation's democratic record. The infrastructure protecting this heritage had not kept pace with its importance — and the cost of inaction was permanent, irrecoverable loss.

01 Passive Data at Existential Risk

Decades of legislative records resided on ageing magnetic tape, optical discs, and legacy hard-drive arrays — media types with documented annual degradation rates. Without active migration, significant portions of Pakistan's parliamentary record faced permanent, irrecoverable loss. The institutions had no validated recovery capability for already-degraded media.

02 No High-Availability Access Architecture

Existing storage offered no redundancy, no failover, and no geographically distributed copies. A single hardware failure — a scenario that had already occurred with minor archives — represented a total loss event. The legislative record had zero meaningful business continuity or disaster recovery posture in place.

03 Hours of Retrieval Blocking Active Governance

Accessing historical proceedings for ongoing legislative work — referencing prior bills, cross-checking testimony, retrieving video evidence for standing committees — required hours or days of manual retrieval from physical media stores. Modern parliamentary operations demand instant, searchable access to the complete record, not warehouse archaeology.

04 Security Posture Inadequate for Classified Material

A substantial portion of the archive contained classified proceedings, in-camera session recordings, and constitutionally sensitive documentation. The storage environment offered no encryption at rest, no access-control auditing, and no chain-of-custody guarantees — a critical compliance gap for Pakistan's most sensitive institutional records.

Technology Stack

Three Pillars of National-Grade Preservation

Each technology was chosen for a specific role — VAST Data for the storage fabric, NVMe-oF for the network transport, and AES-256 encryption for the security layer that makes classified material safe.

Universal Storage Layer

VAST Data All-Flash Platform

VAST Data's Disaggregated Shared-Everything (DASE) architecture separates compute from storage at the hardware level, eliminating the performance bottlenecks and failure domains of traditional storage controllers. The full 800 TB archive is globally deduplicated and erasure-coded across all NVMe drives — every node sees every byte, every node can fail independently without data loss.

DASE architecture — disaggregated shared-everything
200 PB max namespace — NVMe all-flash fabric
NVMe-oF, S3, NFS, SMB multi-protocol support
Network Transport Layer

NVMe over Fabrics Transport

NVMe over Fabrics extends the NVMe command set — originally designed for PCIe-attached local SSDs — across the network, delivering storage performance previously only achievable with directly attached drives. Parliamentary workstations at both Assembly locations access the central VAST archive with the same latency profile as a local NVMe SSD.

Sub-100µs end-to-end latency — 10× lower than iSCSI
65,535 queue depth per namespace
Active-Active multipath HA across both Halls
Security & Compliance Layer

AES-256 Encryption & Access Control

Every object in the 800 TB archive is encrypted at rest using AES-256-GCM — the same standard mandated by NIST for TOP SECRET classification. Key management is handled through a dedicated HSM with FIPS 140-2 Level 3 certification. All access to classified legislative content is governed by role-based policies with immutable audit logs.

AES-256-GCM encryption — HSM FIPS 140-2 L3
SHA-256 integrity verification per object
RBAC + immutable audit trail — zero breaches
